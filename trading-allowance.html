The £1,000 trading allowance explained

The trading allowance lets you earn up to £1,000 a year from small trading or side income without paying tax on it. It is a flat allowance: you do not need to work out profit.

How it works

  • It applies to gross income, so you compare £1,000 with the money you received, before costs.
  • Under £1,000: income is covered, and you normally do not need to report it.
  • Over £1,000: you choose between two methods. Either deduct the £1,000 from your income, or deduct your actual expenses. You cannot do both.

Which method is better?

IncomeExpensesBetter option
£3,000£600Allowance: taxable £2,000 instead of £2,400
£3,000£1,800Expenses: taxable £1,200 instead of £2,000

The break-even is easy: if your real costs are more than £1,000, claiming expenses is usually better.

Separate property allowance

There is a second £1,000 allowance for property income such as renting out a driveway or storage space. It is separate from the trading allowance.
